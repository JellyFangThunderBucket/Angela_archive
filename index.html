Angela built a life around showing up when things get hard.

A nurse, mother, leader and professional fixer of problems, she has spent years becoming the person people look for when the room gets complicated.

By the Excess Staff · Filed 06:00 EDT

There is a normal, respectable way to tell Angela’s story. It begins in emergency medicine, where careers are measured in long shifts, difficult rooms, hard decisions and the ability to keep moving when everybody else has run out of useful ideas.

She began in the emergency department at Danville Regional Medical Center, then continued at Morehead Memorial Hospital, now UNC Health Rockingham. The job titles accumulated because simply being extremely good at one thing was apparently not sufficient.

Charge nurse. Care coordinator. Scheduling. Leadership. The sort of operational problems that begin with somebody saying, “Does anybody know where Angela is?” At some point, being dependable stopped describing a personality trait and started functioning like hospital infrastructure.

She has kept going, kept learning and kept building a life while carrying enough history to make “resilient” sound almost annoyingly understated.

Incident 01 · Rockingham County

Runaway ambulance stopped after Angela stands in road and gives it “the look”

Eden, N.C. — Staff report

A driverless ambulance traveling 71 mph toward downtown Eden came to an immediate and reportedly apologetic stop Tuesday after Angela stepped into the roadway, crossed her arms and displayed what witnesses called “the exact face she makes when somebody schedules twelve nurses for a shift that needs thirteen.”

Transportation officials credited emergency braking until inspection revealed the vehicle had no such system.

Dash audio captured a mechanical tone later interpreted as the ambulance saying, “My bad.”

Angela declined praise and asked why the unit was out of service and whether anybody had called staffing.
